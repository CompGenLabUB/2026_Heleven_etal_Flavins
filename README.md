## Abstract

Essential vitamins are indispensable metabolic cofactors, yet whether
their availability is spatially organized to support local metabolic
demands within regenerative tissues remains largely unknown. Here, we
identify a previously unrecognized flavin-rich cellular network that
spatially organizes vitamin B<sub>2</sub> metabolism during regeneration in
the planarian *Schmidtea mediterranea*. Using endogenous flavin
autofluorescence as a label-free *in vivo* metabolic readout, we show
that this network consists of elongated cells that form longitudinal
varicosity-like strands closely associated with the central nervous
system. RNAi-mediated disruption of riboflavin uptake and metabolism
reduced flavin-associated autofluorescence and its spatial
distribution within the animal, thereby affecting blastema formation,
eye regeneration, and nervous system reconstruction, demonstrating
that riboflavin metabolism is required for successful
regeneration. Single-cell transcriptomics, protein localization, and
functional perturbations further suggest a reciprocal relationship
between flavinocytes and the regenerating nervous system, pointing to
coordinated neuro-metabolic interactions during tissue
repair. Together, our findings demonstrate the importance of
riboflavin metabolism for nervous system regeneration and provide new
insight into the spatial organization of metabolism during tissue
regeneration.
